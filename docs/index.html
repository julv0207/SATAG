Abstract

Recent advances in text-to-audio (TTA) generation have enabled more precise control over the timing of individual sound events. However, generating events with overlapping time intervals can result in inaccurate event boundaries, missing events, or sounds that do not match the requested events. We attribute these problems to the lack of explicit modeling of acoustic superposition when multiple events overlap. To address these problems, we propose SATAG, Superposition-Aware Temporal Audio Generation. SATAG detects temporal overlap and uses a temporally controllable audio generation network to generate event-specific waveforms. It then adjusts their relative amplitudes, combines them through waveform-domain superposition, and applies peak normalization to the final mixture. Quantitative evaluation shows that SATAG reduces onset and offset errors for detected events under overlapping conditions compared with existing temporally controllable TTA models. Subjective evaluation further shows that SATAG improves audio quality MOS from 3.250 to 4.005 and temporal alignment MOS from 3.300 to 4.305 compared with DegDiT.
